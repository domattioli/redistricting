# Districts drawn to keep social ties together

## In short (for people)

For each of 43 states with more than one district, a map was drawn to keep as many friendships between people in different counties inside one district as it could (Meta's Social Connectedness Index, adjusted for distance), then compared with 10,000 neutral county-respecting maps of the same state.

- **Connected and compact** (no less compact than the typical neutral map): in 4 of 43 states the Democratic seat count falls outside the middle 90% of neutral maps in at least one of 2016, 2020 and 2024. Added up, Democratic seats in these states versus the neutral medians: 2016: 208 vs 209 (8 states more, 9 fewer); 2020: 223 vs 225 (6 states more, 8 fewer); 2024: 192 vs 193 (6 states more, 7 fewer). So keeping social ties together while staying compact does not tilt the national result.
- **Ties only** (no compactness limit): in 5 of 43 states; added up: 2016: 213 vs 209 (8 states more, 7 fewer); 2020: 232 vs 225 (9 states more, 4 fewer); 2024: 196 vs 193 (10 states more, 6 fewer). These maps sprawl (they cut far more neighbour links than any neutral map) and lean a few seats Democratic, which is why the compact version is the main one.

Social ties follow migration, party and race, so this shows what such a rule would do; it is not a neutral rule.

## For AI agents (detailed)

Generated by `python -m redistricting_addons.sci_districts` (`--compact` for the first table). Ties kept = percent of cross-county friendship weight (SCI × both populations) inside one district. Neutral = county-weighted ensemble `<ST>.county20.json`; compact = cut edges at most that ensemble's median. D seats: connected map (neutral median); ▲ / ▼ when at least 95% of neutral maps give fewer / more.
